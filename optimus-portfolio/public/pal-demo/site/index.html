RoboCup: take a policy for a walk.

Our BadgerBots team placed 4th out of 14 teams in robot soccer at the RoboCup Asia-Pacific Beijing Masters. As Low-Level Control Co-Lead for UW–Madison’s RoboCup team, I work on locomotion policies, deployment, and hardware recovery.

With several RoboCup teammates training policies, I built the PAL Playground gallery as a central, browser-based space to test-drive and evaluate our work. It helps us compare policies and select the best ones to hand off to the high-level control team for motion planning and gameplay.

Technical details

    
From policy to proof.
Drive: the exported neural policy selects joint actions 50 times per second inside a live MuJoCo simulation. Keyboard, joystick, touch controls, and gamepad inputs change its target velocity.
Replay evaluation: a short guided playback of five genuine recorded tests: forward walking, sidestepping, turning, stopping, and a speed limit test. This is a replay, not a new evaluation. The full saved scorecard and all 228 attempts remain available below the simulation.
Motion gem: rotate and inspect the measured movement map. Its facets connect observed passing commands; the space between them is not a guaranteed capability region.
Run gauntlet: run the actual evaluator locally in your browser. The full circuit takes up to 18 simulation minutes; choose a faster pace in Settings, or start with Quick check. New results are stored only in this browser. These scores compare simulation packages, not hardware performance.
The featured checkpoint has the highest Dependability and Performance scores among Tund-authored, rankable policies in this gallery snapshot. Source: PAL Playground, Gallery branch. Attribution and licenses.
